## WHY does decision policies of the DEX system exist?

The primary purpose of Decision Policies is to ensure human authority and prevent automation overreach. In a system designed for high-autonomy agents, there is a constant risk that the speed of AI execution will outpace human oversight, leading to "alignment drift" or unintended consequences. Policies provide the necessary guardrails and "brake systems" that keep the agents tethered to human judgment.

Policies also exist to provide consistency in conflict resolution. When an agent encounters two contradictory principles or a situation not explicitly covered by a procedure, the Policy layer provides the rules for how to proceed. It eliminates the need for agents to "guess" at the correct course of action, replacing ambiguity with a deterministic escalation matrix. This ensures that the system's responses are predictable and auditable.

Furthermore, policies enable scale. By pre-defining how certain classes of decisions should be handled (e.g., "Always use model X for task Y unless rate-limited"), we reduce the number of times a human must be manually consulted for routine choices. Policies allow us to "automate the boring stuff" while reserving human cognitive resources for high-stakes, Layer 8 decisions. They are the "Rules of Reason" for a hybrid intelligence system.
