## How do we know it works?

Success for the Policy layer is defined by **"Zero Unauthorized Overrides."** If an agent performs an action that should have required a checkpoint or human approval, the policy has failed. Conversely, if a human operator never feels the need to "panic stop" the system because they trust the pre-defined guardrails, the policy has succeeded. Success is a state of "Calm Autonomy," where the human is informed but not overwhelmed.

Another success criterion is **"Escalation Determinism."** When an agent or a human encounters an ambiguous situation, the path to resolution should be immediate and obvious. Success is when the question "What do we do now?" is answered by a direct link to a policy. If the system can transition from "Ambiguity" to "Filter Application" (the 8:3:8 law) without manual debate, the policy layer is performing its function.

Finally, success is measured by **"Audit Traceability."** Every decision made by the system—especially those that involve model switching or resource expenditure—should be traceable back to a specific policy. If an auditor can look at a `CASESTUDY.md` entry and understand *why* a certain path was taken based on the L0 policy layer, then the policy has achieved its goal of creating a transparent, accountable intelligence system.
