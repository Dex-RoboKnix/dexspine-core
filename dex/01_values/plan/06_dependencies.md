## What does core values need?

The Core Values layer has very few external dependencies, as it is the foundation upon which all other layers are built. However, its primary internal dependency is the **8:3:8 Law of Reduction by Addition**. For the values to be effective, there must be a deterministic method for applying them to ambiguous situations. Without the law, the values remain abstract philosophy rather than actionable infrastructure.

Another critical dependency is **Human Authority (Layer 8)**. The values are a distillation of human intent, and their maintenance and periodic review depend on a human being "in the loop." If the human authority is removed or compromised, the values can become stale or misaligned with the evolving needs of the system. The "Grounding Chain" requires a human at the top to validate that the values still reflect the system's irreducible essence.

Finally, the values depend on the **Structural Integrity of the Spine**. The spine's directory structure, file naming conventions, and Markdown protocol are the "vessels" that hold the values. If the spine is corrupted—for example, by inconsistent naming or the introduction of proprietary formats—the values lose their human-readability and machine-parseability, rendering them useless for grounding agents. The values depend on the very infrastructure they define.
