## How do we measure core values?

Measuring the effectiveness of abstract values requires a combination of quantitative and qualitative data. One primary metric is **Value Citation Frequency**. By auditing decision logs, system reports, and pull requests, we can track how often specific core values are cited as the rationale for an action. A high citation frequency indicates that the values are actively guiding the system, while a low frequency suggests they have become "orphaned" from the actual work.

Another metric is **Decision Alignment Consistency**. This involves periodically reviewing a sample of decisions made in downstream layers (Policy, Process, Procedure) and scoring them on their alignment with the core values. This "alignment audit" provides a qualitative measure of the system's integrity. A high consistency score confirms that the "Grounding Chain" is intact and that the values are effectively constraining agent behavior.

Finally, we track **Conflict Resolution Latency**. When the 8:3:8 law is invoked to resolve an ambiguity, we measure how long it takes to reach a "structured 1" output. If consulting the values layer significantly reduces the time spent in "binary deadlock," it proves the values are providing a high "return on addition." We also monitor the "Exception Rate"—the frequency with which values are bypassed or overridden—to identify areas where the values may need refinement.
