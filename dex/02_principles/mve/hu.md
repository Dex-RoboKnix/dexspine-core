## Minimum Viable Essence

Our lived experience with early LLM chat windows taught us the pain of context loss. We remember the frustration of an agent "forgetting" its purpose or hallucinating its way out of a project's constraints. This human pain is what birthed the principle of "Markdown as Wire Protocol"—the realization that if the agent can't *see* the history and the foundation in every turn, it will eventually fail. We value plain text because it's the only thing that survives the session reset.

We also discovered, through trial and error, that "Single Agent Beats Multi-Agent" for core architectural work. When we tried to use swarms of agents to build the first DEX prototypes, they spent more time talking to each other than building. The context was spread too thin. This human insight—that a single, deeply-grounded agent is more effective than a thousand ungrounded ones—is a cornerstone of our principles.

Finally, we feel the "Moral Weight of the Veto." We understand that as systems become more autonomous, the human's ability to say "No" becomes more important than their ability to say "Yes." Our principles are designed to preserve this power at all costs. We are building a system that is meant to serve us, not replace us, and our principles are the "safety locks" that ensure the human remains the ultimate arbiter of meaning.
