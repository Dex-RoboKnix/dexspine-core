## What limits fundamental principles?

The primary constraint on the Principles layer is the **"Rule of Simplicity."** To be effective, principles must be few enough to memorize and clear enough to enforce without ambiguity. If a principle requires a multi-page manual to explain, it is not a principle; it is a policy or a procedure. We constrain ourselves to principles that can be stated in a single, punchy sentence (e.g., "1 Task = 1 File").

Another constraint is **"Universal Applicability."** A principle must apply to the entire DEX system, regardless of the specific project or model being used. If a rule only applies to "React development" or "Python scripting," it is too narrow to be an L0 principle. This constraint ensures that the principles remain part of the "irreducible foundation" and don't become cluttered with transient technical requirements.

Finally, principles are constrained by the **"Human Veto."** No principle can be so rigid that it overrides human judgment in an emergency or unique edge case. While we strive for 100% adherence, the "Layer 8 Veto" is itself a core principle that limits the power of all other principles. This ensures that the system remains flexible and that automation never becomes a "suicide pact" where rules are followed into a failure state.
