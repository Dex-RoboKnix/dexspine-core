## What limits execution patterns?

The primary constraint on the Pattern layer is the **"Rule of Abstraction."** To be a pattern, a solution must be abstract enough to reuse in diverse domains, but concrete enough to provide actionable guidance. If a pattern is too abstract (e.g., "Be consistent"), it provides no value. If it's too concrete (e.g., "Use this specific React component"), it's a procedure, not a pattern. We are constrained to find the "Sweet Spot of Generalization."

Another constraint is **"Internal Consistency."** Patterns must not contradict each other or the underlying layers of the spine. A new pattern for "Parallel Tasking" cannot violate the principle of "Single Agent Focus" or the policy of "Sequential Checkpointing." This constraint ensures that the Patterns layer remains a "Harmonious Extension" of the foundation, rather than a collection of conflicting "good ideas."

Finally, patterns are limited by **"Observed Success."** We do not document "theoretical patterns." A pattern must have been proven effective in at least two separate, successful project tracks before it is codified in the L0 foundation. This constraint prevents "Pattern Bloat"—the accumulation of unproven or speculative structures that don't actually improve system quality. Patterns are the "Battle-Tested Archetypes" of the DEX infrastructure.
